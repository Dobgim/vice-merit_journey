Imagine finishing a chapter of your PhD in Vienna, Graz or Innsbruck, working next to a professor whose papers you have been citing for years. The **Ernst Mach Grant 2027/28** makes that possible: the Austrian Government pays a monthly grant for a research stay of **1 to 9 months**. It is open to researchers from every country outside Austria. Applications close on **1 December 2026 at 23:59 CET**, and the step that takes longest, finding a host supervisor, needs to start this week.

This guide explains who can apply, which documents take the most time, and how to use the weeks you have left. You can also see the key facts at a glance on our [Ernst Mach Grant listing](https://www.meritleaders.com/scholarships/ernst-mach-grant-worldwide-2027-28).

## What is the Ernst Mach Grant Worldwide?

It is a research grant run by the OeAD, Austria's agency for education and internationalisation, on behalf of the Austrian Government. It funds **a research or study stay at an Austrian university**, usually to work with a supervisor on your doctoral or postdoctoral research. All fields of study are welcome.

It is **not** a full degree scholarship. You stay enrolled or employed at your home university, and you come to Austria for a set number of months.

Stays for this call start from **September 2027**, and the OeAD says they cannot be extended, so choose the length of your stay carefully.

## How much is the grant?

You receive a monthly grant for every month you spend in Austria, at the rate the OeAD sets for this call. The grant is meant for living costs, so plan to pay for extras such as flights yourself. The current monthly rate is on the official call, linked from our [Ernst Mach Grant listing](https://www.meritleaders.com/scholarships/ernst-mach-grant-worldwide-2027-28).

## Who can apply?

You can apply if you are:

- a **PhD student** at a university outside Austria;
- a **postgraduate or postdoc** who finished your studies at a university outside Austria **after 30 September 2025** and wants to do research in Austria; or
- a **postdoc working as a lecturer or researcher** at a university outside Austria.

The programme page gives an **age limit of 35**. You cannot apply if you are a regular student at an Austrian university, live permanently in Austria, or did most of your academic education there.

If you are a PhD student, a young lecturer, or a recent master's graduate with a clear research plan, this call deserves a serious look. If you are still in your first degree, it is not the right fit yet. Our [eligibility checker](https://www.meritleaders.com/eligibility) can point you to better options.

## When is the deadline?

Applications close at **23:59 CET on 1 December 2026**. Convert this to your own time zone so you don't get caught out. Results are expected in **early July 2027**, and stays begin from September 2027.

Late applications are not accepted, so aim to submit at least a few days early.

## What documents do you need?

For most applicants, the slow part is not the online form. It is getting letters signed and stamped. Plan for these:

- **A supervision confirmation from a lecturer at an Austrian university.** It must use the OeAD template, with a date, signature and stamp. The supervisor also confirms that your project can be done in English.
- **Recommendation letters.** They can be freely worded, but need letterhead, a date, a signature and the institution's stamp, and must be **no older than six months** when you apply.
- **A valid biometric passport.** ID cards are only accepted for EU applicants.
- **Translations.** English documents need no translation. Documents in other languages need a copy of the original plus an English or German translation.

You do not need an official English test such as TOEFL or IELTS, but you do need enough English to work in your field.

There is **no separate research proposal to upload**. The evaluators judge your plan from what you write in the online form, so every section counts. The OeAD also asks you to **declare any use of generative AI**, including which tools you used and how. Write your own answers and be open about any help you had.

## How do you find an Austrian supervisor?

This step decides most applications: without a host who agrees to supervise you, you cannot apply.

1. Search Austrian university websites for researchers working on your topic.
2. Read two or three of their recent papers so you can mention them sincerely.
3. Send a short, polite email: who you are, your research question, why you chose them, and the months you would like to visit.
4. Follow up once after a week. Academics are busy, and a calm reminder is normal.
5. When they agree, send them the OeAD template straight away so they have time to sign and stamp it.

Write to several researchers at once. Waiting until late November is risky, because supervisors may be travelling or slow to reply.

## Your plan for the next four weeks

### This week: find your host

Shortlist three to five Austrian researchers and send your emails. Check your passport is valid.

### Week two: ask for your letters

Ask two referees for recommendation letters on letterhead, dated and stamped. Give them the deadline and a short summary of your project.

### Week three: write your application

Fill in every section of the online form in your own words. Say what you will do each month and what you will produce by the end of your stay.

### Week four: check and submit

Collect the signed supervisor template, prepare any translations, declare any AI use, and submit before 1 December.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Ernst Mach Grant fully funded?

It is partially funded. The monthly grant covers your living costs during the stay, but extras such as flights are up to you.

### Can master's students apply?

Not while still studying for the master's. You can apply after finishing your studies at a university outside Austria, if you completed them after 30 September 2025, or once you are enrolled in a PhD.

### Do I need to speak German?

No. Your supervisor confirms that the project can be done in English, and English documents need no translation.

### Does the application cost money?

No. The OeAD application is free. If anyone promises you a "guaranteed" grant in return for a fee, walk away.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Starting with the form instead of the supervisor.** Contact supervisors first.
- **Old or unstamped letters.** A letter older than six months can be rejected.
- **A vague plan.** State what you will do each month and what you will produce.
- **A stay that doesn't match your work.** Ask for the number of months your plan really needs.
