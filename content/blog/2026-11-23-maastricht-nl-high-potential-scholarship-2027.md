The **Maastricht NL-High Potential Scholarship 2027** is one of the very few awards that pays a master's student's tuition and living costs in full, and it is open to students from outside the EU/EEA. The date that catches people out is the first one: your master's application must be complete by **10 December 2026**, well before the scholarship form itself closes.

If you want to study in Maastricht from September 2027, this guide tells you what the scholarship covers, who can apply and exactly what to do in the next few weeks. You can see the key facts on our [Maastricht University NL-High Potential Scholarship listing](https://www.meritleaders.com/scholarships/maastricht-university-nl-high-potential-scholarship-2027).

## What does the NL-High Potential Scholarship pay?

The scholarship covers your full tuition fees plus a monthly grant for living costs. For the whole programme, the living-cost part comes to **€20,150** for a one-year master's or **€38,750** for a two-year master's. Health and liability insurance (about €700) is also looked after, and visa costs are covered at cost. Flights are not included.

Only about 18 students a year receive it, and roughly 2% of applicants are successful, so treat it as a highly competitive award and apply for other funding in parallel.

## When is the Maastricht NL-High Potential Scholarship deadline?

There are two dates, and you must meet both:

1. **10 December 2026:** your complete application to the master's programme must be submitted through Studielink, with every required document uploaded.
2. **1 February 2027 (23:59 CET):** the scholarship application form closes.

Do not wait for February. The scholarship is only available to students who have already applied for admission on time, so the December date is the real deadline. Convert both times to your own time zone, and aim to submit a day early.

Decisions are expected by early May 2027, and shortlisted candidates are first invited to an online interview.

## Who can apply?

You need to meet every one of these conditions:

- You hold a **non-EU/EEA nationality** (no EU, EEA or Swiss dual nationality).
- You are **no older than 35 on 1 September 2027**.
- You have a **GPA of at least 7.5 out of 10** (or the equivalent on your grading scale).
- You have never taken part in a degree-seeking higher education programme in the Netherlands.
- You are applying to a **full-time master's programme that takes part in the scheme**.

That last point matters. Not every Maastricht master's is eligible, and some programme pages state clearly that they are excluded. Check the programme's admission page before you invest time in the application.

## How do I apply, step by step?

1. **Choose an eligible programme.** Confirm it appears on the list of participating master's programmes.
2. **Check the programme's admission requirements.** Language test, degree background and any extra documents differ between faculties.
3. **Submit your master's application in Studielink** and upload everything before 10 December 2026.
4. **Complete the scholarship form** once your application is in. Do this while your admission documents are still fresh in your mind.
5. **Prepare for an online interview** in case you are shortlisted.

## How do I make my application stand out?

Selection committees at this level see many students with excellent grades. What separates applicants is **fit and direction**.

Explain why this specific programme at Maastricht, and not a similar one elsewhere, is the right next step. Connect your past study or work to a concrete goal you want to pursue afterwards. Be specific about courses, methods or research groups that attract you.

Your motivation letter should read as your own voice, honest and focused. ## Common mistakes to avoid

- Waiting for the February form deadline and missing the 10 December application date.
- Applying to a master's programme that is not part of the scheme.
- Submitting an incomplete Studielink application, with missing transcripts or language proof.
- Sending a generic motivation letter that could be sent to any university.
- Assuming a high GPA is enough on its own.
